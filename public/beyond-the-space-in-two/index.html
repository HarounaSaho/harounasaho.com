Continuum, 2025

Oil on canvas, 48 × 36 in

A young man sits in a plastic chair at the artist’s grandmother’s compound, deeply absorbed in the world on his phone. He sits between two spaces, the compound around him and the one in his hand, and is both old and new. The masks, spirals and glowing forms around him reflect his inner world, one he overlooks while his attention stays on the screen. Continuum also mirrors the artist’s own life between America and home in The Gambia, and the continuous connection that runs between the two.

What Remains, 2025

Oil on canvas, 24 × 36 in

A grandmother sits wrapped in patterned orange cloth, her hands resting open, as her grandchild stands beside her. Set in a courtyard of blues, the pair hold the stillness of a family portrait. Through them, the artist reflects on his own relationship with his grandmother, and on the meeting of old and new. What Remains asks what gets passed down between generations, and what gets left behind.

Offering, 2025

Oil on canvas, 24 × 36 in

A young man painted in deep blue stands with arms crossed against a field of burning yellow. A mask watches from the upper left, and a peacock turns toward him from the right, a red current sweeping between them. Through the peacock and the young man, the painting plays with the line between beauty and vanity, and with the feeling of wanting something you cannot have. Offering considers what that loss might leave behind, and what might grow from it.
